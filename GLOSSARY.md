# To Spray or Not to Spray

To Spray or Not to Spray analyzes weather observations at multiple heights in an orchard to support an applicator's decisions.

## Language

**Observation height**:
The vertical distance of a weather measurement above the local ground surface. It is distinct from elevation above sea level.
_Avoid_: Elevation when referring to sensor height above ground.

**Multi-height observation**:
A set of weather readings associated with different observation heights at a site and comparable observation times.

**Observed wind profile**:
Wind speeds and directions measured at the available observation heights. It describes those sampled levels, rather than a complete three-dimensional wind field or pesticide trajectory.
_Avoid_: Drift trajectory when referring to wind observations.

**Spray drift**:
The airborne movement of spray droplets outside the intended application target during or shortly after application. It is distinct from vapor drift.

**Vapor drift**:
The movement off target of pesticide that has volatilized into gas. Active ingredient properties, formulation and environmental conditions affect this process.

**Spray release height**:
The height above local ground at which a nozzle releases spray. It is distinct from observation height, tree height and elevation above sea level.

Sources for drift terminology: [Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/pesticides-pest-management/growers-commercial-users/drift-mitigation/management-pesticide-spray-drift.html), [Montana State University Extension](https://www.montana.edu/extension/pesticides/reference/drift.html).
