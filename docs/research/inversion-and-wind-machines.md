# Inversion, frost and wind-machine research notes

Source: research notes supplied by the user alongside the challenge brief. No paper title, experiment citation or source links accompanied these notes. They are organized here as claims to investigate, not independently verified scientific guidance or accepted app rules.

## Inversion formation and movement

The supplied notes describe an inversion as cooler air below warmer air and identify four mechanisms or contributing conditions:

1. Cool-air advection or drainage beneath warmer air.
2. Warm-air advection over cooler air.
3. Shading and hilly terrain, including cold-air drainage.
4. Radiative cooling of the ground around sunset, cooling near-surface air more quickly than air above.

The notes suggest that inversions concentrate airborne pesticide and that a breeze can move it, particularly toward lower terrain. They also suggest radiation-inversion air tends to remain in place. These descriptions need source context, including the distinction between droplets and volatilized pesticide; see the [glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md).

## Frost and wind machines: separate research topic

The supplied notes connect frost with inversions and list heating, wind machines and sprinklers as possible frost-protection methods. They mention drought as a constraint on sprinklers. The general statement that frost is caused by an inversion needs qualification and a source before presentation as a universal explanation.

Wind-machine notes describe mixing warmer air aloft with cooler air below, changing temperatures by a few degrees over a limited height range. They state that machines may be ineffective in extreme temperatures and less effective in winds above approximately **3.7 mph**. All effectiveness claims and numeric cutoffs remain unverified here.

An unnamed experiment reportedly:

- Logged temperature every five minutes for five months.
- Considered a **1 degree Celsius** temperature difference significant for assessing wind-machine use.
- Observed that air at **20 m** can be several degrees warmer than crop-level air in strong inversions.

The notes recommend considering multiple seasons of site-specific measurements. Locate the experiment and check its crop, sensor heights, methods and intended outcome before using its results.

The suggested connection between wind below **3 mph** and possible wind-machine use is an exploratory hypothesis. Frost-protection performance does not establish that running a wind machine makes pesticide application safe. The app does not recommend wind machines or use the experiment's 1-degree threshold: its demonstration inversion flag remains upper temperature greater than lower temperature.
